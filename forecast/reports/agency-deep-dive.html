7. Cross-Cutting Insights

1. Forecast data and SAM data serve different purposes.
Forecasts tell you what's coming (12-18 months out). SAM tells you what's active right now. Interior has 3,101 forecasts but only 163 SAM notices — that pipeline is real, just not yet live. Agriculture has 2,317 forecasts / 59 SAM notices — same pattern. Use forecasts to plan, SAM to execute.
2. The SAM CSV export underrepresents civilian agencies.
Interior, Agriculture, GSA, and HHS combined have only ~340 SAM notices — less than DLA alone (935). This is a data quality issue with the CSV export, not a reflection of actual procurement volume. These agencies use SAM.gov extensively — the CSV simply didn't capture them.
3. SB set-aside rates vary dramatically by agency.
Agriculture: 60% SB (food commodity dominance). Interior: 53% SB (construction + IT). GSA: 44% SB. VA: 13% SB (but 44% SDVOSB). HHS: 29% SB. BOP: 30% SB. The "SB-friendly" agencies are Agriculture and Interior — they actively carve out work for small business. VA favors SDVOSB over general SB.
4. The TBD pipeline is the biggest opportunity.
Across these 6 agencies, there are 3,017 forecast opportunities where the set-aside hasn't been determined yet (VA 513, GSA 155, Interior 1,407, Agriculture 925). Early engagement before the solicitation drops could influence these toward SB set-aside.
