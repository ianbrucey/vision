
Technical Requirements

This artifact extracts the technical requirements — personnel qualifications, security clearances, equipment and material standards, safety requirements, and IT security standards — from GSA RLP No. 6FL0489-3 for leased office space in Port St. Lucie / Fort Pierce, FL. This is a real property lease solicitation, not a services contract; therefore, personnel requirements focus on the qualified professionals who must evaluate, certify, design, and construct improvements to the offered building.

2. Security Clearance Requirements
   Facility Security Level

   The facility is classified at Facility Security Level II (FSL II), as determined by the Government based on client agency mix, size of space, number of employees, use of space, location, site configuration, and public access.
   If an offered building already houses a Federal agency, or if the Offeror is competing on multiple Federal space requirements, the security requirements may be increased and the Offeror may be required to adhere to a higher security standard.

Personnel Security Clearances

    FAR 52.204-9, Personal Identity Verification of Contractor Personnel (Jan 2011) — incorporated by reference. Requires contractor personnel who require routine physical access to a Federally-controlled facility or logical access to a Federally-controlled information system to undergo personal identity verification in accordance with HSPD-12 and FIPS 201.
    GSAR 552.204-9, Personal Identity Verification Requirements (Apr 2023) — incorporated by reference. Supplements the FAR clause with GSA-specific PIV requirements.
    FAR 52.240-92, Security Requirements (Nov 2025) — incorporated by reference. Applicable when the contract may require access to classified information. The solicitation does not explicitly state that classified access will be required.
    Background checks for construction personnel are required as part of the Construction Security Plan (Ex. C, Section VI.C).
    No specific federal personnel security clearance level (e.g., Secret, Top Secret) is stated as a prerequisite for offer submission.

Access to Building Information

    Building information (mechanical, electrical, fire/life safety, security system plans, schematics, computer automation systems, emergency operations procedures) shall be strictly controlled and released only to authorized personnel approved by the Government, managed through an access list and controlled copy numbering.
    The Government may direct that the names and locations of Government tenants not be disclosed in any publicly accessed document or record, including building directories.

4. Safety Requirements
   Fire Protection and Life Safety

   Fundamental Code Compliance: The building shall be evaluated for compliance with the most recent edition of the building and fire code adopted by the local jurisdiction, except that technical egress requirements shall be evaluated based on NFPA 101, Life Safety Code (most recent edition).
   A building with a Certificate of Occupancy indicating full compliance with the International Building Code (IBC) is deemed to comply.
   Fire escapes on the floor(s) where offered space is located shall not be counted as an approved exit stair.
   Interlocking or scissor stairs on the floor(s) where offered space is located shall count as only one exit stair.
   Offeror must verify that the Building meets the Means of Egress, Automatic Fire Sprinkler System, and Fire Alarm System requirements of the Lease, or commit to completing improvements to bring the Building into compliance prior to acceptance of the Space.

Required Fire Protection Systems

    Automatic Fire Sprinkler System: Must be installed throughout the building. The Fire Protection and Life Safety Evaluation (GSA Form 12000) requires documentation of the sprinkler system type, coverage, and compliance status.
    Fire Alarm System: Must be installed per NFPA 72. The evaluation form requires documentation of the system type (manual, automatic, combination), connection to central station/constantly attended location, and coverage.
    Fire Alarm initiating devices (e.g., smoke detectors) used for elevator Phase I emergency recall must be installed per NFPA 72.

Fire Protection and Life Safety Evaluation (GSA Form 12000)

    A Fire Protection and Life Safety Evaluation must be completed by a licensed Fire Protection Engineer and submitted with the offer.
    Part A required for all offers; Part B (detailed narrative report by the FPE) required when offered space is on the 6th floor or higher.
    Reports submitted without the FPE's findings, recommended corrective actions, and code references will be returned without review by the GSA Regional Fire Protection Engineering Office.
    The Offeror must sign the Offeror's Statement of Correction, attesting that all work required to bring the space into full compliance will be completed at the Offeror's sole cost prior to Government acceptance.
    Accepted GSA Form 12000 Part B is valid for 5 years from the noted date, provided no major modifications or construction has occurred.

Structural Safety

    Seismic Safety: Sections 2.03 (Moderate Seismicity) and 2.04 (High Seismicity) are both intentionally deleted from this RLP. No seismic evaluation is required.
    Floodplains: Space offered in the 100-year floodplain must comply with applicable floodplain management and protection requirements; the Government may reject offers in floodplains if practicable alternatives exist.
    Blast Resistance: For new lease construction projects, the Lease includes a paragraph "Security for New Construction" with additional blast resistant measures (Ex. C, Section V).
    Emergency Generator Protection (T.I.): Emergency generators must be protected.
    Securing Air Intake Grilles: Air intake grilles must be secured.

Accessibility

    The Building, leased Space, and areas serving the leased Space must be accessible in accordance with the Architectural Barriers Act Accessibility Standard (ABAAS), Appendices C and D to 36 CFR Part 1191 (ABA Chapters 1–10) and 36 CFR 1190.
    Where ABAAS conflicts with local accessibility requirements, the more stringent applies.

Environmental and Hazardous Materials

    Asbestos: If asbestos-containing materials are present in the Building or offered Space, the Offeror must identify them and provide an asbestos management plan or abatement plan (RLP Section 2.05).
    Environmental Considerations: The Government may perform environmental due diligence including Phase I Environmental Site Assessments, lead-based paint and asbestos surveys, radon testing, and vapor intrusion assessments (RLP Section 2.10).
    National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA): The Government may require NEPA review; the Lessor may be required to provide access and information.
    National Historic Preservation Act: If the Building is 45+ years old or in a historic district, the Government coordinates with the State Historic Preservation Officer (RLP Section 2.11).
    HAZMAT Storage: Hazardous material storage is prohibited within the facility except as specifically authorized and must comply with applicable codes (Ex. C, Section III.B.2).
    Placement of Receptacles and Mailboxes: Trash receptacles, containers, and mailboxes must be placed at least 33 feet from any building entrance unless blast-rated (Ex. C, Section III.B.3).

Location Safety Restrictions (Unique Requirements)

    Buildings must not be located within 1,000 linear feet of daycares, mental health or drug rehab clinics, immigration offices, probation and public defender's offices, or social services offices.
    Offeror must be willing to install fencing to secure parking spaces.
    Offered buildings and space will be evaluated by the agency security team on a case-by-case basis.

5. IT Security Standards
   Building Automation and Control Systems (BACS) Cybersecurity

   Lessors are prohibited from connecting any portion of their building and access control systems (BACS) — including fire/life safety control, physical access control, building power/energy control, electronic surveillance, and automated HVAC, elevator, or building monitoring and control services — to any federally-owned or operated IT network.
   In the event of a BACS cybersecurity incident, the Lessor shall assess the incident, identify impacts and risks, follow the organization's cyber and IT procedures, and immediately inform the Lease Contracting Officer's designated representative (Lease Administration Manager) about incidents impacting federal tenant safety, security, or proper functioning.

Recommended Cyber Protection Measures (Encouraged)

    DHS ICS-CERT cybersecurity guidance and recommendations for industrial control systems.
    NIST Cybersecurity Framework (NIST-CSF) for managing cyber risks.
    DHS Commercial Facilities Sector-Specific Plan (2015) for best practices.
    FIPS 140-2 validated encryption modules for safeguarding sensitive data and login credentials.
    NIST-approved encryption algorithms and secure protocols (TLS 1.1, TLS 1.2, TLS 1.3).
    OWASP Top 10 vulnerability mitigation for web applications.
    CIS Benchmarks or industry-recognized benchmarks for system configuration hardening.
    Enforce Least Privilege access; disable unnecessary services (FTP, Telnet); close unnecessary ports; enforce changing of factory default credentials.
    Use updated antivirus software at all times (Kaspersky-branded products prohibited).
    Discontinue end-of-life (EOL) systems; use only vendor-supported applications/systems.
    Operating Systems must be supported for security updates (e.g., do not use Windows Server 2003).
    Installation and patching shall not alter USGCB (United States Government Configuration Baseline) or tenant agency guidance settings.
    Disallow commercially-provided circuits for building systems; install on protected networks behind enterprise firewalls. Workstations/servers running BACS must not be visible on the public internet.

## Evaluation Criteria — GSA RLP No. 6FL0489-3

### Award Methodology

**Lowest Price Technically Acceptable (LPTA).** Section 4.03, “Award Basis,” explicitly states: *“The
 Lease will be awarded to the responsible Offeror whose offer conforms
to the requirements of this RLP and the Lease documents and is the
lowest priced technically acceptable offer submitted.”* There is no
trade-off between price and non-price factors; technical acceptability
is a pass/fail gate, and award goes to the lowest-priced offer that
passes all gates.

### Price Evaluation Detail (Section 4.04)

The Government will perform a **Present Value Price Evaluation** to determine the lowest-priced offer among technically acceptable proposals:

* Prices per ABOA SF are reduced to a composite annual ABOA SF price.
* Discount rate:  **5% annually** .
* Operating expenses escalated at  **2.5% compounded annually** , then discounted at 5%.
* Free rent is evaluated in the year offered.
* Commission credits subtracted from the first year's gross annual rent.
* Cost components added to the present value calculation include:
  * Government-provided services (if not fully serviced)
  * Lump-sum reimbursable items (not discounted)
  * Parking for Government vehicles (discounted at 5%)
  * Relocation of furniture, telecommunications, replication, and other move-related costs
  * A/E design fees and Lessor's Project Management fees (evaluated as percentage uplift on TI Allowance + BSAC amortization)
  * Overtime HVAC: based on offered hourly rate and estimated **80 hours/year** (discounted at 5%)
* All **required option periods** are included in the evaluation.
* Final metric:  **Present Value Cost per ABOA SF** .

### Additional Evaluation Mechanics

* **Competitive Range (4.01):** The LCO may establish a
  competitive range based on cost/price and other factors stated in the
  RLP. All offerors in the competitive range are given the opportunity to
  submit revised proposals. Those excluded are notified in writing.
* **Negotiations (4.01):** The LCO or designee may
  conduct oral or written negotiations with all offerors in the
  competitive range. Negotiations may address rental price for initial and
  renewal terms, and any other aspect of the offer.
* **HUBZone Price Evaluation Preference (4.02):** A
  HUBZone SBC offeror may receive a price evaluation preference under FAR
  52.219-4, as listed in the GSA 3517 General Clauses. The HUBZone offeror
  may elect to waive this preference on GSA Form 1364.
* **Submission: **All offers must be submitted
  electronically via RSAP (leasing.gsa.gov) by August 10, 2026, 5:00 PM
  Eastern. Paper submissions will not be considered. (3.02)

### Page Limits

No page limits tied to specific evaluation volumes were stated in
this solicitation. The RLP specifies required submittal documents
(Section 3.01, table) but does not impose page or volume limits on any
evaluation-related response.
