## The "Dealer Trap" Nuance

Under the  **Non-Manufacturer Rule (NMR)** , if you win a small business set-aside contract for commodities, the government requires that the item be manufactured by a domestic small business.

* **The Compliant Way (Small Manufacturer):** You find a local certified small business that actually *makes* eco-friendly cleaning chemicals or *assembles* custom medical kits in their own facility. You sign a Teaming Agreement or a Joint Venture with them. **This is 100% legal and compliant.** Your system can bid on set-asides all day long using this setup.
* **The Illegal Way (The Dealer Trap):** You find a local certified small business office that doesn't make anything, but they have a wholesale account with a giant corporation. Even if you form a JV with that small business distributor, the *product itself* still comes from a large business factory. **The SBA will throw this bid out immediately** because the actual creator of the product is a large business.

> **The Golden Rule for Products:** In your database, your network partners for supply contracts must be flagged as the **creators/manufacturers** of the goods, not just middlemen. If they are middlemen, you can only use them on "Full and Open" (unrestricted) contracts or on contracts that have an active SBA Class Waiver.

## Step-by-Step Transaction Blueprint

### Step 1: The Automated Match & Quote

Your system flags the DLA solicitation. The software matches the requirements to TexShield Fabrics' profile in your database.

* Your app pulls TexShield’s pre-forecasted pricing ($30/unit).
* The app automatically factors in shipping costs and overlays your management markup, generating a compliant federal bid at $50/unit.
* You push the final package to TexShield for digital sign-off. They click "Approve," legally binding their price to your bid. **Cost to you: $0.**

### Step 2: The Award & The Contingent Contract

DLA reviews the proposal, loves that it uses a 100% domestic small-business manufacturer, and awards the contract to General Consulting LLC.

* The moment you receive the award, your backend system automatically converts your pre-award MOU into an active **Subcontract Agreement** with TexShield.
* This contract contains a strict **"Pay-When-Paid" clause** (conforming to FAR 52.232-40), stating that TexShield will be paid within 3 to 7 days *after* you receive funds from the government.

### Step 3: Eliminating the Production Cost Burden

You want TexShield to start spinning the machines, but you aren't putting up cash for their raw materials. To eliminate your financial burden entirely, you use one of two standard compliance mechanisms:

* **Option A: The Assignment of Claims Act (FAR Subpart 32.8)**
  You and TexShield execute an "Assignment of Claims." This is a legal notice sent to the government Contracting Officer stating that while *your* company holds the contract, the actual government payments are legally assigned to a dedicated project account. This gives TexShield's bank or their material suppliers the ultimate collateral: a guaranteed government invoice. TexShield can now easily secure their own short-term raw material financing based on your contract win. **Cost to you: $0.**
* **Option B: Independent Capacity**
  Because TexShield is an established manufacturer, they already have raw materials or a commercial line of credit on hand. Because they have a binding federal subcontract from you, they willingly absorb the upfront manufacturing cost because the end buyer is the US Government.

### Step 4: Fulfillment & Inspection

TexShield manufactures the 1,000 pouches.

* Your system generates the military shipping labels (MIL-STD-129 tags).
* TexShield ships the pallets directly from their factory facility straight to the DLA depot.
* The DLA receiving officer inspects the goods, accepts the delivery, and logs into the government portal (WAWF/IPP) to sign the receiving report.

### Step 5: The Prompt Payment Flow

The moment the government signs off on the delivery, your system automatically submits your electronic invoice to the DLA.
