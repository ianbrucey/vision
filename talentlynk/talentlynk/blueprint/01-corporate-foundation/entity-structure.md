# Entity Structure

## Purpose
Define the corporate entity architecture — which legal entities exist, what they're used for, and how they relate to SAM.gov registrations and contract bidding.

## Current Entities (as discussed)

### Justice Quest
- **Role:** Legal Tech & IT Services Entity
- **SAM.gov Primary NAICS:** 541511 (Custom Computer Programming Services)
- **Use:** IT-focused contracts only
- **Status:** Already registered on SAM.gov

### FunLink
- **Role:** Master Management Core (to be repurposed)
- **SAM.gov Target Primary NAICS:** 541611 (Administrative Management and General Management Consulting Services)
- **Use:** Cross-domain local contracts (landscaping, janitorial, maintenance) as a Management Prime
- **Action Required:** Update SAM.gov registration to change primary NAICS

## Entity Strategy

The two-entity split prevents mission overlap and keeps each entity clean for its target market:
- **Entity A (Justice Quest):** Stays narrow — IT and legal tech only
- **Entity B (FunLink):** Broad management prime — bids on any service contract, routing execution to network subs

## Future Entities

### Joint Venture Entities (Per-Set-Aside)
When bidding on specialized socioeconomic set-asides (WOSB, SDVOSB, 8a) where neither Justice Quest nor FunLink holds the certification:
- A new JV entity is formed per SBA regulations
- Certified network partner is the Managing Venturer (51%)
- FunLink/Justice Quest is the Administrative Member (49%)
- JV entity gets its own SAM.gov registration and UEI

## Key Rules & Compliance
- SBA JV regulations: 13 CFR § 121.103(h), 13 CFR § 125.8
- Managing Venturer must hold 51% ownership
- JV must be registered on SAM.gov with its own UEI
- Each JV is typically formed for a specific contract or limited purpose
