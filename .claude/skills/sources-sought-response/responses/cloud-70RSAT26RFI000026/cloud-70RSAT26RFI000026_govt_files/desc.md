I.  BACKGROUND

The United States Department of Homeland Security (DHS) Directorate of Science and Technology (S&T) Office of National Laboratories (ONL) Transportation Security Laboratory (TSL) is seeking information for support services to establish a Screening System Data Sharing Consortium (SSDSC) for the purposes of facilitating collection and sharing of screening system data ranging from non-sensitive to Sensitive Security Information (SSI).

With its staff of federal employees and contractors, including physicists, chemists, engineers, and mathematicians, TSL is internationally recognized for its unique ability to advance detection technology from conception to deployment through applied research, test and evaluation, assessment, and certification testing.

Among its many tasks, TSL conducts research, development, and validation of Transportation Security Equipment (TSE) to assess its effectiveness in detecting and mitigating threats posed by explosive devices and contraband items and materials.  To address evolving security challenges, the DHS Transportation Security Administration (TSA) is transitioning toward an open architecture approach for TS - emphasizing standards-based, interoperable software and hardware components.  This approach enables a broad range of industry partners to develop innovative subcomponents and fosters rapid adoption of state-of-the-art solutions across transportation security systems.  To support this move to open architecture, TSL is establishing the SSDSC to facilitate collection and sharing of screening system data ranging from non-sensitive to SSI.  This initiative aims to accelerate the development and validation of machine learning-enabled threat detection algorithms.  Since TSE vendors require access to large and diverse datasets to reliably train and test their models, a critical element of the consortium is providing authorized developers with secure and efficient access to well-curated data from a variety of data providers.

TSL intends to address this need through the development and implementation of a cloud-based platform to receive, store, curate, protect, and enable authorized access to large amounts of screening system data.  The platform will serve as a centralized repository for data from both developmental systems and deployed TSE, including x-ray Computed Tomography (CT) Explosive Detection Systems (EDS) for both checked bag and carry-on bag screening and millimeter wave systems for screening passengers.  The repository will also contain data sets including augmented and fully synthetic data.  Because of the sensitive nature of the data, the platform will need to comply with rigorous requirements for authorized access and privacy protection.

III.  OBJECTIVES

The objective of this RFI is for TSL to obtain information from industry on solutions for developing a secure cloud platform that supports data ingestion, storage, curation, protection, and secure access to large amounts of screening system data for training and testing of automatic threat detection software.  Respondents should discuss specifically whether algorithm developers would receive access to a cloud-based testing environment (containerized code running on cloud Central Processing Units (CPUs)/Graphics Processing Units (GPUs)) or be permitted to download data for testing on their own platforms.  In both scenarios, respondents must address how data security and integrity would be ensured.

In addition, TSL seeks insight from industry on transitioning transportation security screening data from local Network-Attached Storage (NAS) to a secure cloud platform to facilitate sharing, collaboration, and advanced analytics among SSDSC members and other stakeholders.

The objective of this project, if implemented, is for TSL to establish a flexible, scalable, and secure environment for large-scale data migration, management, and utilization.  TSL does not wish to constrain developers’ approaches with a fixed set of use cases; however, some key objectives intended to guide responses include:

    Migration:  Move screening system datasets - including data collected at TSL, by system developers, from TSE, and augmented or synthetic data—to the cloud, ensuring secure transfer and data integrity.
    Data Management:  Ingest, verify, annotate, redact, set permissions, and version-control new or revised data, including updates such as ground truthing, while maintaining robust audit trails of data access, annotation, and modification.
    Indexing and Access:  Provide flexible mechanisms for efficient indexing, sorting, searching, and access to metadata and datasets, including a description of how access is controlled and data are provided only to approved users.
    Collaboration:  Enable integration and collaboration with SSDSC members and external stakeholders by providing secure APIs for data ingestion, curation, annotation, modification, and access.  Additionally, allow developers to access data for training and analysis, including support for linking to developers’ private cloud environments.  This process may require separate cost allocation, and cost sharing or chargeback mechanisms should be addressed.
    Audit and Compliance:  Track and audit data access, including user identification, access history, and usage metrics, to ensure compliance and operational oversight.  Ensure compliance with classification, privacy, and security standards for data ranging from public to SSI.
    Technical and Continuous Improvement:  Address technical dependencies, scalability, and strategies for continuous improvement, such as version control, monitoring, and updating of data management processes and tools.
    Payment and Accounting:  Describe accounting and billing practices for invoicing parties based on actions such as storage, downloading, or using compute services.  Explain if billing can be separated by action and how fees can be tracked and allocated, including options for the Government to bear all or part of the costs.

While the initial build phase is intended to support the SSDSC as currently envisioned, TSL may expand the scope of the platform to address changing needs or other programs that would benefit from a cloud-based data sharing platform.  The development approach should be flexible and adaptable to accommodate evolving requirements.
